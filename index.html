Welcome to the Kim Group Website!

Based in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at UW–Madison, our research asks a simple question: how can we make energy materials withstand damage and last longer?

The systems we study range from batteries to nuclear reactors, where repeated operation involves open-system reactions that exchange matter with the surroundings, reshaping the material’s microstructure and generating large internal stresses.

We start from the idea that damage is almost unavoidable under such operating environments, but not all damage is equally damaging, and failure is often negotiable. So we engineer imperfections into the lattice, from 0D to 3D, to steer damage toward the forms a material can live with.

To do this well, we study how imperfections and microstructural features determine where damage begins, how it spreads, and whether it remains manageable or leads to failure. To uncover these mechanisms, we combine high-throughput atomistic modeling with autonomous materials synthesis and in operando microscopy and diffractometry.

That understanding is what lets us design materials that are more durable, reliable, and sustainable for next-generation energy technologies.

Research Areas

  • Mechanical behavior of inorganic materials under multiphysical environments (e.g., electrochemical, nuclear):
    • Solid electrolytes, mixed ionic-electronic conductors, metals for batteries
    • Metal-ceramic composites for nuclear reactors
  • AI-driven materials design copilot, integrating:
    • Defect/microstructure-aware materials informatics
    • High-throughput atomistic modeling
    • Robot-assisted autonomous synthesis
    • In operando microscopy and diffractometry
