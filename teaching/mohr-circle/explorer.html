Sign convention (Module 9): θ from x to x′, + CCW. On the circle the shear is plotted upward and a shear that tends to rotate the element clockwise is plotted positive: X′ = (σx′, −τx′y′), Y′ = (σy′, +τx′y′). A CCW rotation θ of the element is a CCW rotation 2θ of X′ about C.

Stress state in the x–y plane
θ = 0.00°

Drag the slider (or press play) to rotate the element. Negative θ = clockwise. The dotted trail is the path of X′ and Y′; at θp the shear vanishes (principal planes), at θs it is maximal.

Strain uses the same equations with σ → ε and τ → γ/2; Mohr's circle for strain plots γ/2 vertically. The pink element is the (exaggerated) deformed shape in the rotated frame: sides stretched by εx′, εy′ and the right angle changed by γx′y′.

Each slider rotates the element about its current x′, y′ or z′ axis, on top of what was done before (the order is the order you move them). The faces are plotted as (normal stress, |resultant shear| on the face), the textbook 3-D diagram, so a point moves continuously and stays on a circle only while rotating about a principal axis. Tick "signed τ" to use the 2-D clockwise-positive rule instead (X′ = (σx′, −τx′y′) etc.): this is only defined for rotation about a principal axis, and the page warns when it is not. Demo: press principal about z′ (τx′y′ → 0, all faces shear-free), then drag θx′ — Y′ and Z′ trace the σ2–σ3 circle while X′ stays at σ1. Without the first step the points leave the circles for the shaded region.

Plane stress state and σz sweep range
σz = 0.0

Plane stress still has a third principal stress σz (τxz = τyz = 0). The three circles are drawn on (σ1,σ2), (σ2,σ3), (σ1,σ3); the in-plane x–y circle is bold. The absolute maximum shear is (σ1−σ3)/2 and leaves the x–y plane as soon as σz moves outside the in-plane circle.

A cantilever solid circular shaft (fixed at x = 0, free end at x = L) carries at its free end an axial force N, a transverse force P (downward) and a torque T. Internal resultants on a section at x (sign conventions of Modules 7–8: +N tension, +V rotates the element clockwise, +M makes the beam "smile"): N, V = P, M = −P(L − x), T. Stresses at a point (x, y, z) of the section by superposition: σx = N/A − My/I; torsion τ = Tρ/J acting tangentially, i.e. (τxy, τxz)T = (T/J)(−z, y); transverse shear τxy = VQ/(It) with Q = (2/3)(r² − y²)3/2, t = 2√(r² − y²) → τxy = −V(r² − y²)/(3I) (Fy = ∫τxydA = −V), and τxz = Vyz/(3I) from the elementary circular-section assumption that the shear stresses on a chord point to the intersection of the boundary tangents. Pick the point in cylindrical (x, ρ, φ) or Cartesian (x, y, z) coordinates, then rotate the element about its own x′, y′, z′ axes (each slider rotates about the current axis, on top of the previous rotations). "Align with (x, t, n)" rotates the element about x by −φ so that y′ = t (tangential) and z′ = n (radial): on the surface this is the plane-stress element of the lecture (σt = σn = 0, τxn = 0) and "principal about z′" then gives the principal planes of that element.

Stress state and allowable stresses
λ = 0.00

The stress state is scaled by a load factor λ. Mohr's circle grows until it first touches a limit: σ = +σt,allow (tension), σ = −σc,allow (compression) or |τ| = τallow (shear). The touching point is a specific plane: the principal plane (normal at θp) for tension/compression, the maximum-shear plane (θp ± 45°) for shear — drawn on the element as the crack / slip line. Lower τallow (ductile metal, τallow ≈ σy/2) to switch the mode to shear.
