A cantilever solid circular shaft (fixed at x = 0, free end at x = L) carries at its free end an axial force N, a transverse force V (downward), a torque T and a bending couple M0 about z. Internal resultants on the section at x (sign conventions of Modules 7–8: +N tension, +V rotates the element clockwise, +M makes the beam "smile", +T about +x): N, V (the same on every section), M = M0 − V(L − x), T. Stresses at a point (x, y, z) of the section by superposition: σx = N/A − My/I; torsion τ = Tρ/J acting tangentially, i.e. (τxy, τxz)T = (T/J)(−z, y); transverse shear τxy = VQ/(It) with Q = (2/3)(r² − y²)3/2, t = 2√(r² − y²) → τxy = −V(r² − y²)/(3I) (Fy = ∫τxydA = −V), and τxz = Vyz/(3I) from the elementary circular-section assumption that the shear stresses on a chord point to the intersection of the boundary tangents. Pick the point in cylindrical (x, ρ, ϕ) or Cartesian (x, y, z) coordinates, then rotate the element about its own x′, y′, z′ axes (each slider rotates about the current axis, on top of the previous rotations). "Align with (x, t, n)" rotates the element about x by −ϕ so that y′ = t (tangential) and z′ = n (radial): on the surface this is the plane-stress element of the lecture (σt = σn = 0, τxn = 0) and "principal about z′" then gives the principal planes of that element.

A cantilever beam of rectangular cross-section (width b along z, height h along y; fixed at x = 0, free end at x = L) carries at its free end an axial force N, a transverse force V (downward) and a bending couple M0 about z. No torque is applied (torsion of a non-circular section is outside the elementary theory). Internal resultants on the section at x (sign conventions of Modules 7–8: +N tension, +V rotates the element clockwise, +M makes the beam "smile"): N, V (the same on every section), M = M0 − V(L − x). Stresses at the point (x, y, z): σx = N/A − My/I with A = bh, I = bh³/12; transverse shear τxy = −VQ/(It) with Q = (b/2)(h²/4 − y²) and t = b, i.e. the parabola τxy = −(V/2I)(h²/4 − y²), largest on the neutral axis (3V/2A) and zero on the top and bottom faces; τxz = 0. The stresses do not depend on z; z only moves the element across the width. Then rotate the element about its own x′, y′, z′ axes; "principal about z′" gives the principal planes of the x–y element.

Stress state and material strengths
Ductile metals yield by slip, so they fail when the shear is large enough — Tresca (τmax = Sy/2) or von Mises (σ′ = Sy) — not when a normal stress reaches Sy. Brittle materials fracture when the largest principal normal stress reaches the ultimate strength. Scale the loads with λ and see which envelope is reached first.
λ = 1.00

The envelopes are drawn in the plane of the in-plane principal stresses σA, σB (plane stress, σ3 = 0). Tresca: the largest of |σA − σB|, |σA|, |σB| equals Sy (hexagon) — in the 2nd and 4th quadrants the absolute maximum shear is in-plane, in the 1st and 3rd it is out of plane. Von Mises: σ′ = √(σA² − σAσB + σB²) = Sy (ellipse), equivalently √(σx² − σxσy + σy² + 3τxy²). Maximum normal stress: σA, σB within [−Suc, Sut] (rectangle). The factor of safety n is the value of λ at which the load line through the origin reaches each envelope.
